Act 1: The Problem — Why Villages Need Smart Pond Planning 0:00 – 0:45
Screen Action: Show http://10.1.75.79:5261/ full screen. Mouse over the title header and the clean interactive map of India.
"Hello everyone! Today, I want to show you a project I built to solve a crucial challenge in rural India: water conservation and farm pond planning.

During the monsoon season, rural areas receive heavy rain, but most of it rapidly washes away as surface runoff, leading to soil erosion and severe seasonal droughts. Earthen farm ponds—or talabs—are vital for rainwater harvesting and recharging groundwater. But manual site selection is fraught with error. Digging on top of a hill yields zero inflow, while digging directly inside a raging flood channel causes embankment washouts.

We built an automated, web-based geospatial decision-support tool that analyzes 3D elevation terrain, maps natural gravity drainage, and recommends the safest, highest-yielding pond locations with exact storage calculations."
Act 2: How the Algorithm Works (In Simple Everyday Words) 0:45 – 1:45
Screen Action: Point smoothly to the soil type dropdown, the map layer options, and the workflow tabs.
"How does our system figure this out? Think of it like this:

1. Rolling Marbles Downhill (D8 Flow): If you drop a marble on sloped ground, gravity rolls it along the steepest path. Our backend divides the land into thousands of cells and routes water flow from every cell to its steepest downhill neighbor.
2. Finding Safe Natural Valleys: The computer finds natural depressions where water gathers. But crucially, it enforces a 200-meter safety buffer away from major river torrents so the pond never washes away.
3. Uphill Catchment Delineation: Once an optimal site is selected, we trace backwards uphill—like drawing an invisible bowl around the pond—to find every square meter of land contributing runoff.
4. The Rain Sponge Formula: Finally, we calculate storage volume using the hydrological Rational Equation: Volume = Catchment Area × Rainfall × Soil Factor. Our system connects directly to live weather APIs to fetch real annual precipitation for that exact location!"

Act 4: Distributed Cluster Architecture & Instant Caching 3:30 – 4:15
Screen Action: Highlight the top status bar: 'Cluster Status: 3/3 Nodes Active'. Click 'Search Village' again and re-query 'Khapri' → point to the instant result in under 90 milliseconds!
"Behind the scenes, we engineered this for production scale:
Heavy matrix calculations would freeze a standard web server. So we built a distributed 4-node cluster! System 1 acts as the Gateway Master and Load Balancer, routing jobs across three backend compute workers on Systems 2, 3, and 4 via Round-Robin. If any worker node crashes, the gateway automatically fails over mid-flight without dropping user requests.

Even better, look at this performance: While the first query for Khapri took 3.5 seconds, re-analyzing it now returns in under 90 milliseconds—a 43x speedup! That's our 24-hour persistent SQLite WAL cache with spatial coordinate quantization in action."
